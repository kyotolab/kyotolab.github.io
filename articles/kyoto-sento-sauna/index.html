Kyoto Lab ／ Field notes

In Kyoto, a good sauna
costs ¥550.

Before you go hunting for a hotel spa, try the bathhouse around the corner. Kyoto’s neighbourhood sento charge ¥550, take no bookings, and are open late enough to drop into on the way back from sightseeing with nothing but a small towel. Here are my three favourites with a real sauna. All three are in Shimogyo, the ward I grew up in.

2026.09.28 3 bathhouses ／ all in Shimogyo Daichi Ninomiya

First, the pitch

The neighbourhood bathhouse is all the sauna you need.

A sento is a public bathhouse: the place people went to wash before every flat had its own bath, and where plenty of locals still go at the end of the day. In Kyoto the price is capped by the prefecture. Since April 2025 it has been ¥550 for an adult. That is a fraction of what a dedicated sauna spa charges.

And yet a handful of them run a sauna close to 100°C with a cold plunge fed by well water. The guy soaking next to you is probably from down the street. It is a different Kyoto from the temples: the ordinary, everyday one.

The three

Three bathhouses, each good at something different.

01 ／ Shimabara

Miyako-yu Shimabara（都湯-島原-）

Shimabara has been a pleasure quarter since the early 1600s, and this bathhouse sits right in the middle of it. It has changed names a couple of times over the years. In April 2025 Miyako-yu, a bathhouse from Zeze in Shiga, took it over and opened it again.

The reason to come: two cold plunges. One is properly cold, around 18°C. The other sits somewhere between 20 and 25°C. If cold water is not your thing, the milder one lets you sink in up to your shoulders and actually breathe. Keep an eye out for the statue of Kannon in the rock bath at the back, handed down from the old bathhouse.

Open
3 p.m. to 1 a.m. ／ closed Wednesdays
Where
Nishishinyashiki, Shimogyo. Walkable from JR Tambaguchi Station
02 ／ Gojo-Omiya

Goko-yu（五香湯）

One street east of the Gojo-Omiya crossing. It is a two-storey bathhouse: the baths are downstairs, and the sauna and cold plunge are up a flight of stairs. Ever since it appeared in the 2021 Japanese TV drama Sa-do (roughly, “the way of the sauna”), sauna nerds have been coming from all over.

Here is the odd part: there is a second door inside the sauna. The front room runs at about 85 to 90°C and is fairly gentle. Open the inner door and you are in a room at around 110°C. Warm up in the front, and go through only if you feel up to it. Afterwards, a cold plunge of hard well water is waiting.

Open
Weekdays 2:30 p.m. to 12:30 a.m. Sundays 7 a.m. to midnight. Holidays 11 a.m. to midnight ／ closed Mondays and the 3rd Tuesday
Where
Kuromon-dori, just north of Gojo, Shimogyo. Right by the Omiya-Gojo city bus stop, or about 10 minutes on foot from Shijo-Omiya Station
03 ／ Takase river

Sauna no Umeyu（サウナの梅湯）

On the Takase river, a narrow canal near Gojo. The bathhouse dates back to the Meiji era (1868 to 1912). In 2015 it was taken over by Yutonami-sha, a group that now rescues old bathhouses around Kyoto, and this was their first.

The building is old, but the sauna is well looked after: around 100°C, with two cold plunges (roughly 14 to 17°C and 18 to 20°C), plus an herbal bath and an electric bath, which gives you a mild tingle and is exactly as strange as it sounds. Lots of travellers come here, so if it is your first Kyoto sento, start here.

Open
2 p.m. to 2 a.m., with morning hours on weekends ／ closed Thursdays
Where
Iwataki-cho, Shimogyo. Walkable from Kiyomizu-Gojo Station on the Keihan line

Getting around

Two in the west, one in the east.

Miyako-yu and Goko-yu are northwest of Kyoto Station, around Gojo-dori and Omiya-dori, close enough to walk between. Umeyu is further east, near the Kamo river.

Coming back from Higashiyama or Gion? Go to Umeyu. Coming back from Arashiyama or Nijo Castle? Pick one of the two in the west. Their days off are nicely spread out, so whatever day it is, at least one of them is open.

First time?

That’s all you need to know.

Men and women bathe separately, and everyone is naked. Nobody is looking, promise. The small towel is for washing and for covering up on the walk between baths.

  1. Pay ¥550 at the front counter. Bring cash; coins and ¥1,000 notes are safest. No towel or soap? You can usually rent or buy them there. Whether the sauna costs extra depends on the bathhouse.
  2. Wash at the shower stools before you get in anything. Your towel never goes in the bath water; people fold it on their head or leave it on the side.
  3. Sauna, cold plunge, rest. Repeat a few times. Rinse the sweat off before the cold plunge. For sauna mats and where to sit, follow the signs on the wall.
  4. Dry off before you step back into the changing room, so the floor stays dry.
About tattoos

Unlike many hotel spas and gyms in Japan, neighbourhood sento are usually relaxed about tattoos. Umeyu and Goko-yu are widely listed as tattoo-friendly with no need to cover up. For Miyako-yu, ask at the counter.
