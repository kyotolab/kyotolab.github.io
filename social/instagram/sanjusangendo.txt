All 1,001 statues of Kannon have only stood together in this hall again since 2018, for the first time in 26 years.

Some of them had been on loan to museums. Today 500 stand on each side of a large seated figure, ten tiers deep, and the last one stands behind it. 124 of them date from the temple’s founding in the Heian period.

The hall is about 120 metres long and takes its name from the 33 bays between its pillars. Shoes off, and no photos inside, so this is one to see with your own eyes.

Go at opening: 8:30 from April to mid-November, 9:00 in winter.
