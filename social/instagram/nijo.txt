Nijo Castle’s famous “nightingale floor” was probably never an alarm.

The palace corridors chirp with every step, and you will often hear that they were built to catch intruders. In fact the sound comes from the clamps and nails under the boards rubbing together, and there is no record that it was designed for security. The likelier story is that the floors started to sing as they aged.

Come for the Great Hall instead. In 1867 the last shogun, Tokugawa Yoshinobu, gathered the domains’ senior retainers here and told them he would hand power back to the Emperor. Tokugawa Ieyasu built the castle in 1603, so it saw both the beginning and the end of the shogunate.

Arrive for the 8:45 opening and go to the Ninomaru Palace before the gardens. No photos inside the palace.
