Spot guide 07

Gion

GION

You cannot tell by looking which lanes you may photograph and which you may not enter.

The rule

Many of the lanes around Hanamikoji are private roads. The local association has banned photography there without permission since 2019, and entry itself since April 2024; the signs name ¥10,000. It is not a legal fine. It is a rule the neighbourhood set and posted.

Hanamikoji itself is a public road, and you can take photos there. Nothing on the ground tells you which is which. The lanes have no gates, and they are paved with the same stone as the main street. If in doubt, stay on the main street.

People in kimono are on their way to work

Gion is a hanamachi, a district where geiko and maiko live, train and work. In Kyoto they are called geiko, not geisha. Someone hurrying down the street in kimono in the evening is on the way to work at a teahouse. Do not block their way or point a camera at them.

For what it is worth, most people in kimono in Gion during the day are tourists in rented kimono.

Better ways to see it

See Miyako Odori. Every April, Gion Kobu stages Miyako Odori in its own theatre. Buy a ticket and watch the dance itself. Dates and ticket sales are announced each year, so check before you go.

Walk along the Shirakawa. A few minutes north of Hanamikoji, the Shirakawa stream runs between willows and old townhouses. The buildings are the same, there are far fewer people, and you never have to wonder where you may stand.

Come at 7am. Morning Gion is an ordinary neighbourhood at delivery and cleaning time. The light is good, and you will not end up photographing people at work.

Where it is

Gion runs along Shijo-dori, with Yasaka Shrine at the east end and the Kamo River at the west. Yasaka Shrine is open at night and charges no admission. In this area, it is the place built for crowds after dark.
