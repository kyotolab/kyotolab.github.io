Spot guide 16

Heian Jingu

HEIAN JINGU SHRINE

It looks ancient, but it went up in 1895. The torii gate is 24 metres tall, and the grounds are so wide that even a crowd does not feel cramped.

When to go

The grounds open at 6:00 and are free. They close at 18:00 from March to September, 17:30 in October and 17:00 from November to February. The square in front of the Otenmon gate is huge, so even with people around it feels far less crowded than other big sights.

The exceptions are cherry blossom season and the Jidai Matsuri festival on 22 October. Both get properly busy.

What to look at

The Shin-en garden around the shrine buildings. Its four gardens, east, central, west and south, cover about 33,000 square metres. The Meiji-era garden maker Ogawa Jihei VII created it, and it became a national Place of Scenic Beauty in 1975. The ponds are fed by the canal from Lake Biwa.

The weeping cherries just inside the entrance appear in novels by Tanizaki Jun’ichiro and Kawabata Yasunari. The garden opens at 8:30 and closes between 16:30 and 17:30 depending on the season. Entry is 600 yen for adults (as listed in June 2026).

Good to know

The shrine buildings date from 1895. After the capital moved to Tokyo, a shrinking Kyoto built them for an exhibition marking 1,100 years since the city was founded, as a five-eighths copy of the main hall of the ancient palace. The shrine honours Emperor Kanmu, who founded the Heian capital.

The great torii over the road is newer still. It went up in 1929, is made of reinforced concrete, and stands about 24 metres tall. When it was built, it was the biggest torii in Japan.

How to fit it in

It is about 10 minutes’ walk from Higashiyama Station on the Tozai subway line. The Okazaki area around it has art museums and a zoo.

Nanzen-ji and its aqueduct are within walking distance. Walk the Philosopher’s Path from Ginkaku-ji in the morning, see Nanzen-ji, then spend the afternoon at Heian Jingu and Okazaki: a whole day on foot.
