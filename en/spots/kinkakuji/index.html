Spot guide 05

Kinkaku-ji

KINKAKU-JI

About 20 minutes. Look at the ground floor before the gold.

When to go

The visit is a one-way loop of about 20 minutes, and you cannot go inside the building. Unless you arrive first thing, several tour coaches land together around 11am and the railing becomes a queue.

Because the visit is short, it should not be the centre of your day. Plan it as your first stop.

What to look at

Look at the ground floor before the gold. The ground floor is not gilded. It is plain wood and white plaster.

Each of the three floors is built in a different style: the ground floor in the aristocratic shinden style, the second in the samurai style, the third in the Zen style. Only the second and third floors are covered in gold. Ashikaga Yoshimitsu built it as a villa in 1397, and it became a temple after his death.

Good to know

The pavilion you see today was rebuilt in 1955. It burned down in an arson attack on 2 July 1950 and was rebuilt in its original form five years later. The 1987 restoration used thicker gold leaf than the original.

Its formal name is Rokuon-ji. “Kinkaku-ji” is the common name, but the signs and the bus destinations use it.

How to fit it in

There is no train station nearby; people usually go by city bus. The buses to Kinkaku-ji are among the most crowded routes in Kyoto.

Ryoan-ji and Ninna-ji lie to the south-west along the same road, and the three fit into half a day. Pair it with Ginkaku-ji and you cross the whole city from one side to the other.
