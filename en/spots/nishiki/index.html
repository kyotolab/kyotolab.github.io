Spot guide 08

Nishiki Market

NISHIKI MARKET

Eat in front of the shop you bought from, and hand the stick back.

The one rule

Visitors to Nishiki Market are asked not to eat while walking. The street is narrow, with people flowing both ways. People who eat as they walk move slowly, stop suddenly, and hold skewers and cups at the height of other people’s clothes. And this is still a food market that sells fish and pickles unwrapped.

Once you buy something, eat it in front of that shop, and give the stick and rubbish back to that shop. Many shops have a place to stand and eat, or a bin. There are no bins on the street.

When to go

Shops open around 9:30 to 10, varying by shop. Many close in the late afternoon; it is not a place for the evening.

On a weekday morning you see Nishiki still working as a market. On a weekend afternoon it is a queue with a roof over it.

What to buy

Choose things you cannot get elsewhere: pickles sold by the single slice, fresh yuba, sesame tofu, ingredients for dashi, knives from specialist shops, and seasonal Kyoto vegetables you will not see in a supermarket.

More shops take cards now, but it still varies by shop. Carry a little cash.

Where it is

One block north of Shijo-dori, running east–west between Teramachi-dori and Takakura-dori: about 390 m long, about 126 shops, and around 400 years old. It has long been called “Kyoto’s kitchen.”

At the east end is Nishiki Tenmangu, a small shrine squeezed between the arcade’s shops.
