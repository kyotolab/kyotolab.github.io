Spot guide 19

Kyoto Botanical Gardens

KYOTO BOTANICAL GARDENS

Come up out of the subway and you are at the gate. It opened just over 100 years ago as Japan’s first public botanical garden.

When to go

It is quietest on weekday mornings, right after the 9:00 opening. It gets busy on weekends in early April, when the cherry trees bloom, and in mid-November for the autumn leaves.

Open 9:00 to 17:00, last entry 16:00. Closed 28 December to 4 January. Hours can change with the season or events, so check the official information before you go.

What to look at

Start with the big glass conservatory. Your entry ticket covers it, and it is open 10:00 to 16:00 (last entry 15:30). Even on a rainy day, you can enjoy the garden in here.

In autumn, head for Nakaragi Forest, the natural woodland on the north side. Around 500 trees, including maples, ginkgo and dawn redwoods, turn red and gold from mid-November to early December. In spring, around 500 cherry trees reach full bloom in early April.

Good to know

It opened in 1924 as Japan’s first public botanical garden. After the war it was taken over by the Allied forces and reopened in 1961. It covers about 24 hectares and holds around 12,000 kinds of plants.

Admission is 500 yen for adults, 250 yen for high school students and visitors aged 65 and over, and free for junior high students and younger. It is run by Kyoto Prefecture, not the city, which is why its Japanese name says “Prefectural”.

How to fit it in

Take the Karasuma subway line to Kitayama Station; Exit 3 is right by the gate. It is about 15 minutes from Kyoto Station with no change.

The Kamo River runs along the west side of the garden. When you leave, walk south along the riverbank. The Nakaragi-no-michi path by the river is lined with weeping cherries in spring. Keep going for about 40 minutes and you reach the Tadasu no Mori forest of Shimogamo Shrine.
