Spot guide 09

Nijo Castle

NIJO-JO CASTLE

The floors squeak, but probably not to catch intruders. Come for the hall where the last shogun gave up power.

When to go

The castle opens at 8:45, and last entry is at 16:00. The main sight, the Ninomaru Palace, is a one-way route that you walk in socks along narrow corridors. Once tour groups fill them, you barely move. Go straight to the palace, before the gardens.

The inside of the Ninomaru Palace is closed every Tuesday in January, July, August and December, and over the New Year. The castle grounds can be open while the palace is not, so check the day if you visit in those months.

What to look at

The Great Hall of the Ninomaru Palace. In 1867 the 15th shogun, Tokugawa Yoshinobu, gathered the senior retainers of the domains here and told them he would return power to the Emperor. Tokugawa Ieyasu built the castle in 1603, so it saw both the beginning and the end of the shogunate.

The palace corridors chirp when you walk on them. They are called the “nightingale floor” and are usually described as an alarm against intruders. In fact the sound comes from the clamps and nails that hold the boards rubbing together, and there is no record that it was built as a security feature. The more likely explanation is that the floors started to squeak as they aged.

Good to know

Photography is not allowed inside the palace. Most of the paintings on the sliding doors and walls are replicas; the originals are kept in the castle’s painting gallery and shown in rotating exhibitions.

The inner Honmaru Palace reopened in September 2024, the first time in 18 years. It needs a reservation and a separate ticket. The castle became a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1994 as part of the Historic Monuments of Ancient Kyoto.

How to fit it in

The east gate is right outside Nijojo-mae Station on the Tozai subway line, one of the few major sights you can reach without a bus.

Allow at least an hour for the palace and gardens. The Kyoto Imperial Palace is about 20 minutes’ walk east, so Nijo Castle in the morning and the Imperial Palace after lunch works on foot and by subway.

The images are original illustrations, not photographs. Opening hours, prices and rules change, so please check the official information before you go.
