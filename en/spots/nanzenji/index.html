Spot guide 12

Nanzen-ji

NANZEN-JI

The water running over the brick aqueduct flows north and becomes the canal along the Philosopher’s Path.

When to go

It is open 8:40 to 17:00 from March to November and to 16:30 from December to February. Entry closes 20 minutes before, and the temple is closed from 28 to 31 December. No booking is needed.

The grounds and the aqueduct are free to walk. The Sanmon gate, the abbot’s hall and Nanzen-in each charge their own fee. Early morning is the quietest time; only the autumn-leaf season is in a different league of crowds.

What to look at

The Sanmon gate is 22 metres high; the present gate was rebuilt by Todo Takatora in 1628. Climb to the upper floor to see Kano Tan’yu’s ceiling paintings of celestial beings and phoenixes. The famous story of the outlaw Ishikawa Goemon admiring the view from here comes from a kabuki play; Goemon died more than 30 years before this gate was built.

The abbot’s hall is a National Treasure. Its front garden, known as “tiger cubs crossing the water”, is attributed to Kobori Enshu.

The aqueduct

Walk on past the gate and a row of red-brick arches appears. This is the Suirokaku, an aqueduct built in 1888, about 93 metres long and 13 metres high. It carries the canal that brings water from Lake Biwa to Kyoto across the temple grounds. The engineer was Tanabe Sakuro.

Water still runs over it, and it flows north to become the canal beside the Philosopher’s Path on the way to Ginkaku-ji.

How to fit it in

It is about 10 minutes’ walk from Keage Station on the Tozai subway line.

Walk the Philosopher’s Path about 2 km north and you reach Ginkaku-ji. In either direction it makes a half day with almost no buses. The Heian Shrine is also close by.
