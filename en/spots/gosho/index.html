Spot guide 10

Kyoto Imperial Palace

KYOTO GOSHO

Free, and no booking needed. Emperors lived here for about 500 years.

When to go

You do not need to book. Since 2016 the palace has been open all year without an application. Enter by the Seishomon gate on the west side, where bags are checked.

Hours change with the season: 9:00 to 17:00 from April to August, to 16:30 in March and September, and to 16:00 from October to February. Last entry is 40 minutes before closing. It is closed on Mondays (the next day if Monday is a holiday), over the New Year, and on days of official events.

What to look at

The Shishinden, the main hall where emperors were enthroned. The one you see was rebuilt in 1855, deliberately in the style of the Heian-period palace. It looks ancient, but it dates from the very end of the Edo period.

In front of it stand a cherry tree on the left and a tachibana citrus tree on the right, as in the old court. You cannot go inside the buildings; the route takes you around them.

Good to know

Emperors lived here for about 500 years, from the enthronement of Emperor Kogon in 1331 until the court moved to Tokyo in 1869.

Photography is allowed, but not tripods or drones, and there is no eating or smoking. The surrounding Kyoto Gyoen is a public park of about 65 hectares that you can walk through at any time. In the Edo period it was packed with some 140 residences of princes and court nobles.

How to fit it in

The Seishomon gate is about 5 minutes’ walk from Imadegawa Station on the Karasuma subway line. Marutamachi Station also works.

Nijo Castle is about 20 minutes’ walk to the west. Nijo Castle in the morning and the Imperial Palace after lunch works on foot and by subway.
