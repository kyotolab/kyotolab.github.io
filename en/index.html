Why

The rules were never
written down.

Almost every awkward moment between Kyoto and its visitors comes from the same place, and it is not rudeness on either side. The rules here were never written down, because for four hundred years everyone already knew them.

A bathhouse posts a sign in English and people still stand in the changing room, unable to move. A lane in Gion looks public and is not. A market asks you not to eat while walking, and the request reaches almost nobody. Translating a sign does not fix this. There is no sign to translate.

So we write the thing down — plainly, in the languages people actually read — and where writing is not enough, we build the tool. The founder grew up in Shimogyo, left for six years, and came back to a city he had to learn again.

Spot guide

The hour changes
the place.

Kyoto’s famous places all fill up at the same hours. But the place is not what is uneven — the hour is. The same spot at 7am and at 1pm are two different places. What is worth telling you is not just where to go, but when, and what to look at.

Practical

How to use
a bathhouse.

A sento is not a spa and not a hot spring resort. It is the neighbourhood bath, and the order of operations is the whole thing. We wrote it as a sequence you can follow without reading Japanese — and left a copy with the bathhouse, so the staff can point at it.

Open the guide ›
01Shoes off at the door, into the locker
02Pay at the desk, take a small towel
03Undress completely in the changing room
04Rinse before you get in — this is the one
05Sit to wash. Never in the bath
06Small towel stays out of the water

Menus and signs

Say it once, properly

Allergens, stock ingredients, and the things that are not obvious from a photograph — written so a diner can check before ordering.

  • Four languages as standard
  • No app for the customer to install

Getting found

An address that works abroad

Kyoto's street-name addresses are the clearest system in Japan and almost unusable in a foreign map app. We fix the pin, not the address.

  • Map and rideshare entries
  • A short arrival note you can send
  • Photographs of the actual door

Walk-ins

Answer without English

"Can you cut my hair now?" is a lost sale when there is no way to say yes or no. A card at the counter handles it in seconds.

  • Open or booked, at a glance
  • Waiting time without speaking
  • Printed, not another screen
